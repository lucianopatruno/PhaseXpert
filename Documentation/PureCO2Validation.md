# Pure-CO₂ validation

## Status and scope

This milestone checks the native CoolProp adapter against published
experimental values. It does not validate CO₂ mixtures, phase envelopes or the
IFE Model, and it does not authorize a production accuracy claim.

The tests execute through `ProviderRegistry` and the native iOS bridge. They
therefore check unit conversion, provider selection, C/Swift interoperability
and CoolProp output together. A build without the locally generated
XCFramework skips these experimental cases and keeps CoolProp unavailable.

## Scientific implementation

CoolProp 8.0.0 documents the following formulations for carbon dioxide:

- density and equilibrium thermodynamics: Span and Wagner (1996),
  DOI `10.1063/1.555991`;
- dynamic viscosity: Laesecke and Muzny (2017),
  DOI `10.1063/1.4977429`.

Agreement with those formulations is implementation verification, not
independent experimental validation. The selected acceptance cases therefore
come from experimental publications rather than values generated by CoolProp.

## Experimental reference sets

### Density: capture and dense-pipeline conditions

Mantilla et al., “P-ρ-T Data for Carbon Dioxide from (310 to 450) K up to
160 MPa,” *Journal of Chemical & Engineering Data* 55 (2010) 4611–4613,
DOI `10.1021/je1001158`.

The source reports pure-CO₂ measurements from a single-sinker magnetic
suspension densimeter and less than 0.03% relative experimental uncertainty
over most of its range. Five points within PhaseXpert's current input domain
are retained from the NIST ThermoML representation.

The absolute acceptance tolerance was declared before executing PhaseXpert:

`reported expanded uncertainty + 0.1% of the experimental density`.

The additional 0.1% is the source's reported maximum scale of deviation from
the Span–Wagner equation at elevated pressure. The terms are added
conservatively rather than combined statistically.

### Low-density viscosity: capture conditions

Schäfer, Richter and Span, “Measurements of the viscosity of carbon dioxide at
temperatures from (253.15 to 473.15) K with pressures up to 1.2 MPa,”
*Journal of Chemical Thermodynamics* 89 (2015) 7–15,
DOI `10.1016/j.jct.2015.04.015`.

Four gas-phase values and their expanded uncertainties are retained from the
NIST ThermoML dataset. The tolerance is the greater of the reported expanded
uncertainty and 0.5% of the measured value. The 0.5% floor conservatively
encloses the source's stated 0.20–0.41% expanded relative uncertainty and
rounding/state sensitivity in the published values.

### Cold dense viscosity: ship and dense-pipeline conditions

Chapoy, Owuna, Burgass and Ahmadi, “Viscosity of the CO₂ + CH₄ Binary Systems
from 238 to 423 K at Pressures up to 80 MPa,” *Journal of Chemical &
Engineering Data* 69 (2024), DOI `10.1021/acs.jced.4c00083`, CC BY 4.0.

Five independently measured pure-CO₂ points between 220.05 K and 280 K and
between 8.26 MPa and 28.32 MPa are retained. The tolerance is the greater of:

- twice the reported combined standard experimental uncertainty; and
- 4% of the measured viscosity, matching the source's stated uncertainty for
  the reference CO₂ viscosity correlation in this region.

## Acceptance rules

- Tolerances are fixed in source before PhaseXpert output is inspected.
- A failure is investigated; a tolerance is not widened merely to pass.
- Tests must exercise the registered native provider, not a mock engine.
- Values, units, property status and provider availability are asserted.
- Unsupported or unavailable providers must never return substitute values.
- Passing these cases retains the label **Preliminary — validation pending**.

## Limitations and next evidence

The selected data do not establish phase-boundary accuracy or behavior in the
critical region. They also do not validate impurities. Further review requires
an IFE-approved comparison against REFPROP under an appropriate licence and
additional published phase-equilibrium and mixture measurements.

The experimental teqp pure-CO₂ provider reuses the Mantilla density subset for
its narrow density-only native bridge validation. The teqp-specific values,
deviations and current iOS XCTest infrastructure status are documented in
`Documentation/TeqpIntegration.md`.
